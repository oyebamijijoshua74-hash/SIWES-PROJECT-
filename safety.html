Incident Response Protocols

If you suspect compromise, click a scenario to review immediate containment and recovery steps.

  1. Disconnect from Network Immediately: Disable Wi-Fi and unplug Ethernet to cut off any active command-and-control communication with the attacker's server.
  2. Change Passwords from Another Device: From a clean, uncompromised device (e.g., your phone on mobile data), change the password for the affected account and any others sharing it.
  3. Terminate All Active Sessions: Use the account's security dashboard (Google, Microsoft, Bank) to sign out of all other active sessions.
  4. Review MFA Devices and Email Rules: Verify the attacker has not added a foreign MFA device, recovery email, or email forwarding filter.
  5. Run a Full Malware Scan: Use Windows Defender or Malwarebytes in offline mode to scan for any dropped payloads.
  1. Identify Reused Accounts: Stolen credentials are immediately fed into credential-stuffing bots. If you used that password on 10 websites, all 10 are now at risk.
  2. Generate Unique Replacement Passwords: Use our Password Generator to create a distinct 16+ character password for each service.
  3. Enable TOTP-Based 2FA: Add Time-based One-Time Passwords (Google Authenticator / Authy) to all financial and primary email accounts.
  4. Monitor Financial Activity: Watch card transactions closely and sign up for breach notification alerts on HaveIBeenPwned.com.
  1. Isolate Instantly: Disable Wi-Fi and disconnect from all local networks immediately to prevent malware from propagating to shared drives or other devices.
  2. Do Not Pay the Ransom: Law enforcement agencies advise against paying. Payment funds criminal operations and provides no guarantee of a working decryptor.
  3. Boot into Safe Mode: Start the OS in Safe Mode with Networking disabled to prevent malicious auto-run services from loading.
  4. Restore from Air-Gapped Backups: Wipe the drive, reinstall the OS, and restore data from offline or versioned cloud backups not connected during the infection.
  1. Initiate Official Account Recovery: Visit the platform's official recovery page (e.g., instagram.com/hacked) and use identity verification to reclaim access.
  2. Revoke Third-Party App Permissions: Audit and revoke OAuth access for any unrecognized third-party applications connected to the account.
  3. Alert Your Contacts: Attackers frequently message your friends requesting emergency money or SMS codes. Notify people via an alternative channel to disregard these messages.
Hardening Principles

Device & Network Defense

Device
Endpoint Hardening
Enable full-disk encryption (BitLocker on Windows, FileVault on macOS). Set your screen lock timeout to under 3 minutes when in public or campus environments. Disable auto-run for removable media.
Network
Public Wi-Fi Hygiene
Public networks in airports and cafés can be cloned as "evil twin" hotspots. Always use a reputable VPN or your phone's mobile hotspot for banking, student portals, and any sensitive tasks.
Data
The 3-2-1 Backup Rule
Maintain 3 total copies of critical academic and work data, on 2 different media types (e.g., SSD + cloud), with 1 copy stored offsite or in versioned cloud storage.
